LARy: building a cumulative map

No single year is an all-time extent. Use independently selectable source layers, then build a separately verified cumulative layer. The two converted GIS snapshots are useful starting data; they do not cover every historical alignment.

Status: incomplete historical coverage. The raw combined file contains 96 source features with overlap. The application displays a derived comparison: muted gold for shared stretches, long teal dashes for 1915-only stretches, and slate blue for 1930s-only stretches.

Scope assumed: passenger corridors used by LARy and its Yellow Car successors through 1963, including documented temporary alignments. Keep predecessor-only lines, freight spurs, yards and individual parallel tracks as separately scoped additions. “LARy company only” would require excluding successor-era additions.

Separate layers and combined overlay

Brooks and Lutz’s public research GIS contains a 1915 mixed-operator file and a 193x-LArail file. The conversion keeps 59 records marked LA from 1915 and all 37 records from the latter. These are source features, not route totals. The data appendix, printed pages 69–70, identifies a 1935 reference map; the exact snapshot date of the 193x geometry remains unverified.

Why a pair of years is insufficient

Earlier losses, intermediate temporary construction, and later terminal additions all matter. The events below are a targeted review of ERHA route histories, not an exhaustive chronology. Each linked event still needs geometric reconciliation; dates should be corroborated with contemporary maps or notices. A service reroute over existing track does not add a physical corridor.

This combination is an audit strategy, not proof that every track is covered. Mixed rail/bus maps require checking the legend.

How the eventual merge should work

  1. Keep raw source layers immutable and individually selectable.
  2. Audit early-only, intermediate temporary, late terminal and optional facility segments into separate correction layers.
  3. Choose canonical geometry for each physical corridor; split at junctions and realignments. Keep source IDs and observation dates.
  4. Reconcile positional differences manually before snapping; close parallel lines may represent real alternatives.
  5. Combine accepted segments into a separate cumulative layer. Retain references to all contributing observations; dissolve only genuinely shared geometry.
  6. Store observed_on separately from opened_on, closed_on and track_removed_on. Route transfers do not necessarily mean construction or abandonment.
  7. Mark unresolved segments and unknown dates explicitly. Claim completeness only after every route-history change and selected facility scope is accounted for.

Suggested selectable layers: 1915 source, 1930s source, early corrections, temporary alignments, later additions, and verified cumulative corridors. Keep facilities optional. The correction and verified cumulative layers have not yet been drawn.

The app uses a derived 1915 + 1930s comparison. The classifier splits stretches using a five-metre tolerance and a maximum twenty-degree direction difference in UTM 11N. Crossing lines do not automatically match. Shared display geometry comes from the 1930s source, with exact duplicate paths dissolved within each display class. All classified source observations are retained with their original attributes and hidden from rendering. Geometric matches remain approximate: near-parallel tracks can match, and larger digitizing offsets can appear unique. Historical gaps still require review. The blue/orange SVG above remains the original unclassified source overlay.
